Sunlight zone · 50 m

Green sea turtle

Its family has crossed these waters for over a hundred million years. Resting, it can stay under for hours, then rise, unhurried, for a single breath.

Sunlight zone · 100 m

Bluefin tuna

Warm-blooded and never still: a tuna must keep swimming to breathe. It can burst past 60 km/h, and dive into water barely above freezing.

Twilight zone · 600 m

Crown jellyfish

Red is invisible here, because no red light ever reaches this depth. Under attack it spins a ring of blue light around its bell, a burglar alarm for bigger predators.

Twilight zone · 900 m

Giant squid

Up to thirteen metres long, with eyes as wide as dinner plates, thought to have evolved to catch the faint glow of a sperm whale drawing near.

Midnight zone · 1,000 m

Lanternfish

Each species wears its own pattern of lights. By night, billions rise toward the surface to feed, then sink again by dawn: the largest migration on Earth.

Midnight zone · 2,000 m

Deep-sea anglerfish

The lure is a lantern of glowing bacteria. In some species the tiny male bites onto the female and fuses with her for life.

Midnight zone · 3,000 m

Gulper eel

A mouth larger than the rest of its body. The whip-thin tail ends in a small light that flickers pink, drawing prey toward the jaws.

The Abyss · 4,000 m

Tripod fish

It stands on three stilt-like fin rays, facing into the current with its feelers raised, waiting for food to drift into its mouth.

Hadal zone · 8,000 m

Hadal snailfish

The deepest fish ever filmed swam at 8,336 metres. Below about 8,200, the chemistry of a fish's cells gives out, and the amphipods take over.
