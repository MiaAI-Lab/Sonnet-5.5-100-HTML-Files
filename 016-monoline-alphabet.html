      Construction

      One em is a square of 100 units. Capitals stand 70 units tall between a cap line at y 15 and a baseline at y 85; figures share the same height. Round forms are circles of radius 17 to 46 (B, C, D, G, J, O, P, Q, R, U, 0, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9), with the S built from two slightly widened ellipses. The steep diagonals of A, V, W and 7 lean 17° to 25° from vertical; N, X, Z and K open up to 35°–48° so their counters stay clear.

      Each glyph is a short list of strokes written as SVG path data: straight segments and circular or elliptical arcs, nothing fitted. Stroke order follows how a hand would write the letter.

      Rendering

      Strokes are drawn on with stroke-dashoffset, one after the other, with a small ink dot riding the tip. Terminals are round, square or butt; corners stay round, or mitre until the angle turns sharp, then bevel.

      The wobble slider resamples every stroke at 2-unit intervals and pushes each point with value noise, then redraws it at eight frames a second, so the line boils like a hand-inked animation. Copy SVG exports exactly what you see.

      Spacing & set

      Each glyph carries its own side-bearings — 2 to 5 units for diagonals, 9 for stems — plus a kerning table for diagonals and open letters, plus 0.7 × the stroke weight so heavy weights do not collide. Lowercase input maps to the same capitals.
