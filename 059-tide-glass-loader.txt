==================== TITLE ====================
Tide: A Glass That Loads

==================== DESCRIPTION ====================
A single tall glass tumbler, drawn in layered inline SVG, stands on a pale aqua table under a soft wall gradient, with a slim tap peeking in from the top edge. As a simulated, uneven 96 MB download proceeds (stalls, bursts, a long last stretch), the tap pours a thread of water that thickens in bursts, sputters into single drips when it stalls, and raises a two-layer wave: a semi-transparent turquoise front surface over a deeper teal back surface, each a sum of two sines. A damped spring "slosh" driven by pointer position, device tilt and the fill rate leans and ripples the surface. Bubbles rise and pop as rings, a lemon slice bobs and tilts with the surface slope, and a coral-striped paper straw leans in the glass (bent by refraction below the waterline). The percentage is set as large as fits in a heavy slab serif inside the glass, drawn twice and clipped by the exact water shape: ink-teal above the waterline, white below it. Microcopy under the glass reads Pouring, stall and burst lines, Almost there, and Cheers!, with a live MB counter. At 100% the waves settle, the lemon segments spin, a diagonal sheen and sparkles sweep across, the numerals become "Cheers" with an optional glass clink, and a coral "Pour again" button appears to drain the glass and start a new random download. Caustic light patches shimmer on the table and grow with the water level.

==================== TECHNIQUES ====================
- Layered SVG glass: thick base, tapered walls drawn as an even-odd ring, specular streaks, condensation droplets, elliptical rim front and back, contact shadow and light pool.
- Two-sine water surfaces rebuilt each frame as SVG paths (front turquoise, back deep teal), a bright foam line, a meniscus that climbs the glass walls and a tilt term from the slosh variable.
- The same water path is reused as a clipPath, so the numerals are drawn twice (dark outside, light inside) and the straw is drawn again refracted below the surface.
- Damped spring slosh: substepped second-order spring fed by pointer x, pointer velocity, deviceorientation gamma and fill rate; drives tilt, wave amplitude and lemon drift; settles at 100%.
- Uneven download model: a hand-authored first-run plan of stalls and bursts, then randomised plans on replay; progress follows wall-clock time with smoothing, so slow devices finish on schedule.
- Particle systems in SVG: bubbles with wobble that pop as expanding rings, splash droplets from the stream, drips from the tap while stalled.
- Two-tone numerals auto-fit by measuring text length against the glass width at mid-height, with digit-count snapping and a spring pop on the final word.
- Procedural caustics: a low-resolution canvas evaluating an iterated sine-warp caustic function, masked to the glass's light pool, drawn a few times a second and scaled up smoothly.
- Finish choreography: settling waves, lemon spin with friction, a masked diagonal sheen sweep, rotating four-point sparkles.
- Optional WebAudio glass clink (partials plus a noise tick), created only after the sound toggle is pressed, low master gain.
- Robust layout: SVG letterboxing maths keeps the tap, table horizon and light pool aligned to the glass at every size.

==================== INTERACTION ====================
The loader starts by itself and shows percentage, MB counter and microcopy that changes with the download's mood. Moving the pointer left and right tilts the water (and fast movement sloshes it); on phones, tilting the device does the same. Tapping or clicking the glass gives it a nudge and, if sound is on, a soft clink. The speaker button in the corner (or the M key) turns the optional clink on; sound is off by default and only starts after that gesture. When the glass reaches 100% it celebrates and the "Pour again" button (or the R key) drains the glass and begins a new, differently uneven download. The progress element exposes aria-valuenow and value text for assistive technology. With prefers-reduced-motion the slosh, waves, caustic drift and light shafts are much calmer or static, the entrance animations are removed, and the loader still completes normally.
