I The hour

The hour

At ten past dusk the streetlamps are lit but the sky has not agreed to leave. The garden exhales. Bergamot, still cold from the knife; neroli, white as a held breath; and under everything, cedar and musk, warm as a coat someone left on the back of a chair.

Composed in Grasse

One long August, the windows open, forty-one drafts. We kept the one that smelled like the moment you stop noticing you are wearing anything at all.

II The pyramid

Three acts of evaporation

A perfume is not a sentence. It is a sunset — it changes as it goes. Touch any note and the whole hour leans toward it.

Top notes first fifteen minutes

Sicilian peel, cold from the knife. Pink pepper cracked over it like a dry, bright laugh in the next room.

Heart notes two to four hours

Orange blossom, honeyed at the edges and green at the stem; night-blooming jasmine, creamy and a little indecent.

Base notes until the next morning

Pencil shavings and a shelf in the sun; white musk like laundered linen, the quiet after everyone has left.
