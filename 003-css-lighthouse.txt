==================== TITLE ====================
Cape Verity Light

==================== DESCRIPTION ====================
A dusk portrait of a fictional 1874 lighthouse, drawn entirely with divs, gradients, clip-path, masks, filters and box-shadows, with no script in the file. A deep-teal sky (#0d2b3e) melts through teal and dusty mauve into a coral-peach horizon (#ff9a76) where a low sun sliver and a distant ship sit. Above a craggy islet stands a cream-and-signal-red (#d64933) tapered tower with a gallery, railing, a glowing lantern room and a domed red roof; beside it a whitewashed keeper's cottage with a flickering window, a chimney that breathes smoke, and a fluttering red flag. A blurred conic-gradient beam sweeps across the sky once every eight seconds (Fl(2) W 16s, as the small caption says) and lights the drifting clouds it crosses, while a glare blooms in the lantern as the beam faces the viewer. Five rows of scalloped waves, cut by radial-gradient masks, slide at different speeds and directions with foam-lit crests; a buoy blinks, gulls glide with occasional flaps, and the rocks wear warm sun rim light on their left edges and cool sky light on their right. A brass lever at the corner lights the lamp: the sun sinks, the sky deepens to polar navy, a crescent moon and more stars appear, windows light up and the beam becomes blinding. The bottom caption reads CAPE VERITY LIGHT · est. 1874 in spaced old-style serif small caps, with a light-characteristic line beneath. A whisper of film grain and a vignette finish it.

==================== TECHNIQUES ====================
- Everything is scaled from --u (1vmin) with horizontal anchors in percent and vertical anchors in a --H variable (100dvh), so tower, lantern, beam origins and cloud masks stay locked together at any aspect ratio; portrait media queries scale the scene up
- Box-shadow stars: four one-pixel elements carrying long hand-placed box-shadow lists (tiny, medium and bright groups with blur and spread), each twinkling on its own opacity keyframes, plus a night crop that fades in
- Clouds from tiles of overlapping radial-gradient ellipses that drift by compositor-only translateX; the same tile is reused as a mask on a warm cream copy that contains a counter-translating, rotating conic-gradient beam, so clouds light up exactly where the beam crosses them without any script
- Beam: a conic-gradient wedge with a length mask and blur, rotating about the lantern with sine-eased keyframes and a fade at both ends; a synchronised radial glare pulses when it points at the viewer
- Tapered striped tower with clip-path, repeating-linear-gradient bands, cylinder shading and a warm sun edge; gallery, corbels, railing, lantern glass with mullions and Fresnel rings, ribbed red dome and finial
- Waves: five rows built from radial-gradient masks (a dome row plus a solid body), foam rings and crest highlights as background layers, slid by whole tiles for seamless loops, at different speeds and directions with bobbing wrappers
- Rocks as clip-path polygons with faceted gradients; rim lighting from chained drop-shadow filters (warm and cool) whose alpha and colour cross-fade to moonlight at night
- Brass lever from conic, radial and linear gradients: engraved arc groove, screws, spring-eased rotating arm, a bulb that lights; a real hidden checkbox and label supply the :checked state, and the sibling combinator drives a --night variable that fades every layer with CSS transitions
- Lantern hover and keyboard focus: an invisible button over the lantern sets a --boost variable through a sibling selector, which brightens the beam, glare, lit clouds and lantern glass
- Gull silhouettes as single clip-path polygons with a scaleY flap and a bobbing wrapper; flag flutter, smoke, foam, buoy blink and irregular window flicker keyframes
- Inline SVG feTurbulence film grain in overlay blend, a vignette, and an old-style serif small-caps caption with hairline rules

==================== INTERACTION ====================
Pure CSS. Hover the lantern (or Tab to it; a cream focus ring appears) and the beam, glare, cloud lighting and glass all blaze brighter, then relax when you leave. Click or tap the brass lever at the bottom right (top right on phones), or focus it with Tab and press Space, and the checkbox behind it toggles between dusk and night: the arm springs across its arc, the bulb lights, the label changes from "Light the lamp" to "Trim the lamp", the sun slips below the horizon, the sky and sea deepen over about three seconds, a crescent moon, extra stars and a moon path on the water fade in, the tower windows glow and the beam sharpens. Click again to return to dusk. Everything else loops on its own: the beam sweep, drifting clouds, sliding waves, gulls, ship, smoke, flag, blinking buoy and flickering window. With prefers-reduced-motion the loops run four times slower, the flicker, blink and water glints stop, and transitions shorten, but the scene still looks complete.
