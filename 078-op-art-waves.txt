==================== TITLE ====================
Vibration No. 4

==================== DESCRIPTION ====================
An interactive op-art study that fills the whole window with fine lines and lets your eye do the rest. Two canvases are stacked: the first draws about 474 horizontal lines bent by three travelling sine waves and a lens-like Gaussian bulge; the second draws the same field with slightly different phases on an oversized canvas that is rotated by CSS between roughly 0.5 and 3 degrees and blended with difference (or multiply for Riley), so that moire bands, fingerprint rings and glinting seams evolve continuously and seem to pulse and shimmer. Two further patterns share the same engine: Checker Warp, a black-and-white checkerboard whose corners ride a sine field and the bulge, and Concentric Eyes, two offset systems of rings that interfere into radial spokes. Three colourways swap the mood: Monochrome ink on paper, Riley with alternating cobalt and vermilion lines on cream, and Acid fluorescent lime on black. The interface is deliberately Swiss and tiny: museum-label plates in a grotesque sans set in hairline frames, a caption reading "VIBRATION No. 4 — 1,024 lines" whose number is the live count of lines actually drawn, and a compact control panel with chips and hairline sliders.

==================== TECHNIQUES ====================
- Two stacked canvases (software 2D contexts) with the second one oversized, rotated by CSS transform and composited with mix-blend-mode difference (XOR-style moire for Monochrome and Acid, using an inverted second layer) or multiply (Riley).
- Displacement math built for speed: three travelling sines expanded with the angle-addition identity so each point needs only multiplies, plus a separable Gaussian bulge (one exp per column and per row).
- Midpoint-smoothed quadratic curve chains so each of the roughly 474 lines per layer stays smooth with only about 90 to 190 segments across the width (fewer on phones).
- Checker Warp: displaced corner grid filled as one batched path per colour class, with a difference-blended inverse checkerboard as the moire layer.
- Concentric Eyes: two ring systems with wobbling radii, pulsing outward, centres orbiting each other under pointer influence.
- Pointer-driven bulge centre and swell with exponential easing, an autonomous Lissajous drift when the pointer rests, pointer speed feeding an energy term.
- Per-theme density and thickness tuning (Riley and Acid use coarser lines so colours stay distinct and the lime glows).
- Intro amplitude envelope so the straight lines bloom into waves, cross-fade when pattern or colour changes, difference-blended custom ring cursor.
- Museum-label UI plates with theme-aware CSS variables, tabular numerals, hairline sliders, live count caption.
- Pause when the tab is hidden, redraw skipped while paused and still, reduced-motion mode slows speed and fixes the layer angle.

==================== INTERACTION ====================
Move the pointer (or drag a finger) to steer the bulge centre; moving faster or pressing swells it, and when the pointer rests for a few seconds the bulge drifts on its own path. Scroll the wheel (or trackpad pinch, or a two-finger pinch on touch) to change line frequency. A click or tap on the artwork cycles the pattern: Waves, Checker Warp, Concentric Eyes. Keys: Space pauses or resumes, M cycles pattern, C cycles colour, R resets the sliders. The panel offers chips for pattern and colour, sliders for frequency, amplitude, speed and thickness with live readouts, and a Pause button; on phones the panel is tucked behind a Controls button. With prefers-reduced-motion the speed starts at a quarter, the moire layer angle is fixed and transitions are instant, while the image remains fully drawn.
