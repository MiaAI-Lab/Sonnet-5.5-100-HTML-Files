==================== TITLE ====================
MERCURY — Liquid Chrome

==================== DESCRIPTION ====================
A full-screen pool of liquid mercury with one word floating over it. A WebGL fragment shader looks down at the metal from a pitched perspective camera and shades a slowly heaving surface built from a domain-warped, three-octave value-noise height field with analytic derivatives, a drifting broad swell, a heavy gaussian finger dent with a raised meniscus, and up to eight expanding ring-wave impacts. The normals reflect a procedurally generated photo studio (a long warm strip light, an overhead soft-box, a vertical tube light, a cold blue rim strip, a cold horizon gradient and a gunmetal dome) through Fresnel, edge-growing chromatic aberration and a faint oil-slick film of #7df9ff, #ff7ad9 and #ffe27a that appears only where the metal turns away. The result is graded into the palette: gunmetal shadows, silver mid-tones, ice highlights. Over it hangs the single huge word MERCURY, drawn as wide-tracked, thin monoline SVG capitals in champagne #e8d9b5 with mix-blend-mode: difference, so it turns to inked midnight over blazing highlights and glows champagne over dark metal; each letter draws itself on after a first droplet lands. Tiny mono captions sit in the corners inside dark glass chips ("Hg · 80 · 200.59 u", "Liquid at room temperature", a live impact and swell readout), inside a hairline plate frame with champagne corner brackets. On portrait phones the word turns vertical like a book spine. Without WebGL, layered rotating conic-gradient chrome takes over. The mood is cold, heavy, exact and a little dangerous.

==================== TECHNIQUES ====================
- Full-screen triangle fragment shader with a pitched perspective camera and analytic ray/plane intersection, so ripples foreshorten into ellipses and the far field compresses
- Domain-warped fbm height field using value noise with quintic interpolation and analytic derivatives (three octaves, rotated lattices), so surface normals cost one evaluation instead of finite differences
- Up to eight impacts passed as vec4 uniforms (position on the plane, start time, strength; a negative strength means droplet): each is a dispersing ring packet with 1/sqrt(r) attenuation and a dent that rebounds, scaled with distance from the camera so it reads at any depth
- A continuous "heavy finger": gaussian dent plus meniscus rim, sized by depth so it looks constant on screen, following the pointer with damped smoothing; an invisible Lissajous finger and periodic impacts keep the metal alive when nobody is touching it
- Press-and-hold swell that multiplies the whole height field, then releases with a large ring wave; short press or tap drops a tighter, higher-frequency droplet; Space and hold on the keyboard do the same
- Procedural studio environment built from smooth-box functions in azimuth and elevation, sampled three times per pixel (R, G, B) with an outward offset for chromatic aberration that grows toward the screen edges
- Fresnel weighting, an oil-slick cosine-cycle palette blended by grazing angle, filmic tone curve, then a gunmetal-silver-ice gradient map, vignette and per-pixel dither
- Monoline SVG lettering with pathLength-normalised stroke-dashoffset draw-on, set in mix-blend-mode: difference against the live canvas; rotated to vertical in portrait
- Adaptive render scale (0.75 on high-DPR displays, a frame-time governor that can lower it further), context-loss handling, visibility pause, delta-time animation
- CSS fallback: two counter-rotating blurred conic-gradients plus drifting radial highlights, and a custom difference-blended cursor ring whose label reads finger, press or swell

==================== INTERACTION ====================
Mouse: move over the metal and a heavy finger presses a dent that follows the cursor, shedding expanding rings every so often as it travels; a small ring cursor labelled "finger" trails it. Press and it deepens ("press"). Click quickly to drop a droplet that splashes and sends tight ripples outward. Press and hold for a quarter of a second and the ring grows and reads "swell": the entire surface heaves higher and darker, the HUD swell readout climbs toward 100%, and on release a big ring wave rolls out. Touch: a finger on the screen is the finger; a tap is a droplet; a long press swells; a drag drags the dent and sheds rings. Keyboard: tap Space for a droplet at a random spot, hold Space to swell. Idle: after about two and a half seconds without input an invisible finger drifts across the surface on a Lissajous path, dropping a soft impact every few seconds. The bottom-right caption changes with what you do: a surface-tension fact at rest, a joke about toxicity when many impacts are alive, and a density fact during a swell. Intro: the metal fades up, the first droplet lands, the letters draw themselves in one by one, then the frame and captions appear. With prefers-reduced-motion the time base runs at 40%, the invisible finger is slower and rarer, the letters and captions appear immediately, and the fallback rotates far more slowly; touch and pointer effects stay available because the user asked for them.
