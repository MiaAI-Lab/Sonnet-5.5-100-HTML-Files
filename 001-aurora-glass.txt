==================== TITLE ====================
Tromsø, 69.6°N — Aurora Glass

==================== DESCRIPTION ====================
A single-screen travel-and-weather dashboard for Tromsø floating on frosted glass above a living northern-lights sky. Behind everything, a WebGL fragment shader paints up to seven sinuous aurora curtains (green, teal, violet and a rare rose) with a razor-bright lower edge, fine field-aligned rays and soft under-glow, over a polar-night gradient from #040914 to #0b1633. A 2D canvas adds a twinkling star field with a faint Milky Way, jagged midpoint-displacement mountain ridges with snow caps and green rim light, a scatter of town lights on the far shore, and a lone lit ice-fishing cabin with a stargazer and tripod. The frozen lake below mirrors the aurora and the mountains, blurred and rippled, with glinting ice streaks. On desktop, four glass cards flank an open central view of the sky and the huge light-weight title "Tromsø, 69.6°N": an Aurora forecast card (Kp arc gauge with a gradient stroke and glowing thumb, plus a 12-hour visibility sparkline with a hover read-out), a Tonight card (−7 °C, 18% cloud cover, a drawn waxing-crescent moon at 23% lit), a Best viewing spots list with distance chips, and a "Tap to intensify" slider card. On tablets and phones the cards stack over the fixed sky. Card borders, glows and highlights are tinted live by the colours currently dominating the aurora. Type is a light geometric sans with tabular numerals and +0.18em uppercase micro-labels. The tone is calm, cold, patient and a little wondrous.

==================== TECHNIQUES ====================
- Full-screen WebGL fragment shader (0.7 render scale, adaptive governor) with hash-based value noise, per-ribbon sinuous edges from summed sines plus noise, coverage and brightness masks, perspective-leaning rays, an exp() fall-off profile and screen-style tone mapping; up to 7 ribbons whose count fades in and out fractionally with the slider
- The same aurora function sampled in mirror (blurred rays, ripple displacement, ice glints, depth fade) for the frozen-lake reflection, so sky and lake share one pass
- Layered 2D canvases: star field with twinkle and a faint Milky Way, ridge silhouettes built by midpoint displacement with snow-cap gradients, rim light, blurred mirrored reflections, town lights, a cabin with lit window, a stargazer and tripod
- Glassmorphism cards: backdrop-filter blur(24px) saturate(160%), layered gradient fills, inset highlights and inner glow, a masked 1px gradient border, a runtime-generated grain texture, and a pointer-following specular sheen
- Live aurora tint: JavaScript computes the drifting weights of the four aurora colours, feeds them to the shader, and writes them to CSS custom properties (--a-rgb, --b-rgb) that colour card borders, glows, chips, the slider thumb and the hero numerals
- Pointer physics: smoothed pointer position and velocity feed the shader as position, energy and push, so curtains bend along the motion and brighten around the cursor; clicking bare sky sends a brief intensity pulse
- 3D tilt with perspective(), max 6 degrees toward the pointer for each card, with damped springs; an idle wandering light keeps the glass alive without a pointer
- SVG data widgets built at runtime: 240-degree Kp arc gauge (pathLength normalised dashes, gradient stroke, colour-matched thumb), Catmull-Rom smoothed sparkline with gradient fill, a "now" marker and a hover/keyboard tooltip, and a moon-phase path computed from the illuminated fraction
- Custom-styled native range input (gradient track with unfilled veil, glass orb thumb, ticks) plus a seven-bar ribbon indicator
- Staggered CSS entrance choreography using the individual translate and scale properties so it never fights the tilt transform; runtime fake clock, live dot ping, toast, and a shooting star with trail and glow on the S key
- Graceful degradation: no-WebGL gradient fallback with drifting blobs, context-loss handling, reduced-motion mode, visibility-based pause, delta-time animation with clamped steps

==================== INTERACTION ====================
Move the mouse (or drag a finger): the aurora curtains bend along the direction of motion and brighten around the pointer, and every glass card tilts up to 6 degrees toward the cursor while a specular sheen and a lit border segment follow it. With no pointer present, a soft light wanders across the glass on its own. Drag the "Tap to intensify" slider, click its track, or use the arrow keys, Home and End: aurora intensity, the number of ribbons (3 to 7), the Kp gauge (1.0 to 9.0, with G-scale wording), the sparkline amplitude and the seven-bar ribbon indicator all follow smoothly. Hover or touch the sparkline (or focus it and press the left and right arrows) to read the visibility for any hour. Click a viewing spot to select it and the caption below updates with the drive time. Tap or click empty sky for a short intensity surge. Press S (or use the Shooting star button, which also serves touch users) to launch a shooting star with a toast; one also appears on its own every minute or so. Entrance: the hero fades in, then the cards rise from below one after another. Keyboard focus shows clear aurora-coloured outlines. With prefers-reduced-motion the aurora drifts at a quarter speed, stars stop twinkling, tilt and the automatic shooting stars are disabled, the entrance becomes a plain fade and the live-dot pulse is removed; the page still looks complete. Data shown is illustrative and labelled as such in the footer.
