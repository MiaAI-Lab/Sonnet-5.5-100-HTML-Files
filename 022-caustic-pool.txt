==================== TITLE ====================
Deep End: A Sunlit Pool

==================== DESCRIPTION ====================
A full-screen WebGL pool seen from directly above on a bright afternoon. One fragment shader paints the whole scene: a white-and-pale-blue tile floor with grout, bevels and per-tile colour variance; six swimmer lane lines with T-marks and painted lane numbers (lane 4 in sun yellow); two layers of the classic iterative sine caustic riding on top; and a water surface whose slopes (five travelling waves, pointer wakes and click splashes) refract everything beneath it. Depth runs from a bright, pale aqua shallow end to a saturated turquoise-to-#0a6f9a deep end, with softer caustics and dimmer floor detail as it deepens. Sun glints sparkle where wave slopes match the sun, with a warm glare in the top-right corner. A giant bubble-outlined "DEEP END" (white rounded outline with a coral drop shadow, drawn in an offscreen canvas) is painted on the floor, wobbling with the same refraction and blurring and dimming as it sinks toward the deep side. A shaded inflatable coral-and-white ring and a glossy flamingo float (signed-distance shapes with rounded-volume lighting, seams, wings, beak and eye) bob, drift, bump into each other and get pushed by splashes and swimming pointers. Tiny rounded-caps signage labels ("Lane 4 · 27°C", sun and depth tags) and a Calm / Breezy / Choppy switch sit on the water. Without WebGL a CSS fallback with animated radial-gradient caustics, tile grid, lane lines, a striped ring and outlined lettering appears.

==================== TECHNIQUES ====================
- Single full-screen-triangle WebGL1 fragment shader (GLSL ES 1.00) with highp check, compile/link errors reported via console.warn and a CSS fallback
- Procedural tile floor: hashed per-tile tint, anti-aliased grout and bevel light/shade, lane lines and T-marks via mod() and smoothstep
- Water surface as analytic slopes: five directional sine waves with domain warp, high-frequency sparkle waves, up to 16 pointer-wake ripples and 6 splashes evaluated as Gaussian-windowed ring wave packets with analytic derivatives
- Refraction: the floor, lane lines and painted text are sampled at an offset proportional to the surface slope and to depth; text uses a shallower offset so it stays legible
- Two-scale, two-speed iterative sin-based tileable caustic, domain-warped to hide tiling, attenuated with depth
- Beer-Lambert-style absorption plus scattering colour that blends #00b8c8 into #0a6f9a along a depth direction (horizontal on landscape, diagonal on portrait)
- Text and lane numbers drawn to an offscreen 2D canvas as rounded bubble outlines (glyph grown by round-join strokes minus a smaller growth), uploaded as a texture and blurred with a 5-tap kernel that widens with depth
- SDF-shaded floats: torus ring with striped stylised shading; flamingo built from ellipses, a tapered 10-segment Bezier neck, smooth-min blending, finite-difference normals, specular, wing arcs, beak and eye; floor shadows and a lapping-water halo
- Splash foam ring, crown core and radial droplets computed procedurally; specular sun glints and glare
- Adaptive render scale from measured frame time (with ?res= override), rAF delta-time loop, pause when the tab is hidden, pointer-events with capture and a hold-to-charge cannonball ring

==================== INTERACTION ====================
Move the pointer (or drag a finger) across the pool to leave a wake: a moving dimple plus a trail of small ripples whose strength follows speed, which also nudges the floats. Click or tap to drop a splash with expanding rings, foam and flying droplets that shoves the ring and flamingo away; press and hold to charge a shrinking yellow ring and release for a bigger cannonball splash with an echo. Space, Enter or S drops a splash at a random spot for keyboard users. The Calm / Breezy / Choppy buttons ease the wave height, refraction and glints. The floats drift on their own, bob with the swell, spring gently back to their lanes and bump each other. With prefers-reduced-motion the water animates at 40% speed, defaults to Calm, and ripples are softened; the scene remains fully interactive.
