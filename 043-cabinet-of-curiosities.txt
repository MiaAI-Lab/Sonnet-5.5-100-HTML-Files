==================== TITLE ====================
Cabinet of Curiosities: Coleoptera

==================== DESCRIPTION ====================
A single engraved parchment plate, shown on a dark walnut desk under a slanting beam of lamplight. The plate has a triple rule with gold inner line and cinnabar corner studs, a small-caps "Plate XIV" and a large italic "Coleoptera" title, and eight hand-drawn beetles in two rows: a golden dung scarab, a verdigris ground beetle, a vermilion jewel beetle, a long-horned sentinel, a stag beetle with antlers, a seven-spotted ladybird, a gilt leaf beetle and a banded sexton beetle. Each has a hand-lettered figure number, an italic Latin name with small-cap genus, its common name, a length in millimetres and a scale bar drawn to true proportion, and a brass entomology pin. A brass magnifying glass with a wooden handle drifts on its own between specimens until the visitor takes it; inside the glass the plate is magnified 2.2 times and reveals a hidden layer: cross-hatching, punctured striae, raised ridges with shadow lines, tiny hairs on legs and antennae, eye facets, an iridescent sheen and marginal notes about who collected each beetle. Clicking a specimen pins it and lays a taped paper field card on the desk with habitat, range, season, a note and a tiny beetle whose legs wiggle. A moth crosses the page now and then and dust motes glitter in the beam.

==================== TECHNIQUES ====================
- Parametric beetle builder: each species is a data object (head, pronotum, elytra outline parameters, antenna type, mandibles, colours, pattern) turned into cubic-bezier paths for the right half, drawn twice with a mirror transform for perfect bilateral symmetry.
- Three jointed leg pairs per side with tapering strokes, joint dots and claws; antennae in five types (lamellate, filiform, serrate, clubbed, very long).
- Elytra patterns clipped to the wing-case shape: striae, ridges with gold edges, spots, bands, gold stripes, gloss streaks; metallic gradient plus a sheen overlay.
- True magnifying lens: the whole plate is generated a second time with a detail flag, scaled 2.2 times about the lens centre with a matrix transform and clipped by a moving SVG clipPath circle; the brass rim, handle, glass tint, glint arcs and drop shadow are drawn as SVG.
- Parchment painted once on a canvas (value-noise mottling, fibres, foxing blots and specks, edge browning, water ring, centre fold) and embedded as an image in both layers.
- Hand-lettered labels using per-letter rotate and dy jitter in SVG text; small caps via font-variant.
- Layout switches between a landscape 4x2 and a portrait 2x4 plate, always fitted to the viewport.
- Field card with CSS paper texture and tape, mini specimen whose legs animate with SVG animateTransform; entomology pins with a springy drop when toggled.
- Ambient canvas overlay: lamp beam, 70 dust motes that glow inside the beam, and a moth drawn with flapping bezier wings.

==================== INTERACTION ====================
Mouse: the lens follows the pointer with a little inertia. Touch: drag and the lens floats above the finger so it stays visible; tap a specimen to open its card. Click or tap a specimen (or focus it with Tab and press Enter or Space) to pin it and open the field card; Escape or the close button dismisses it. The "Unpin all / Pin all" brass button shows or hides the entomology pins. With no input for a few seconds the lens wanders from specimen to specimen by itself. A moth flutters across roughly every 20 seconds. Reduced motion: the lens rests on the first specimen and follows instantly, no moth, no drifting dust, no card or pin animation.
