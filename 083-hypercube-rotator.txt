==================== TITLE ====================
Tesseract 4D Rotator

==================== DESCRIPTION ====================
A black-blue instrument panel for looking at four-dimensional shapes: a full-screen canvas draws the tesseract, 5-cell, 16-cell, 24-cell and a Clifford torus as hairline edges and glowing vertex dots that shift through ice-blue, magenta and amber with their depth in the fourth dimension, while the three W-plane rotations fold the object inside out. The title TESSERACT is drawn as monoline hairline lettering with a subtitle that changes with the chosen polytope, six fine-line sliders with dot handles set the speed of each rotation plane, toggles switch on additive translucent faces, cell-by-cell highlighting and manual drag mode, a faint axes gizmo and colour legend sit at the bottom, and a Shadow inset shows the fixed projection along W (cube within cube for the tesseract) with the hovered vertex ringed and its 4D coordinates read out.

==================== TECHNIQUES ====================
- Polytopes built from first principles at start-up: tesseract from bit patterns, 5-cell from simplex coordinates, 16-cell from signed axis vectors, 24-cell from all permutations of (+-1,+-1,0,0) with edges by distance, triangle faces by clique search and octahedral cells by dot-product with the 24 dual centres, torus from (cos a, sin a, cos b, sin b)/sqrt2
- Six sequential Givens rotations (XY, XZ, YZ, XW, YW, ZW) applied to a preallocated Float32Array, then 4D perspective projection from w, a 3D yaw/pitch view, and 3D perspective to the screen, with no per-frame allocation in the hot path
- Edge colour, width and alpha derived from the w-depth through a 64-step ice to magenta to amber ramp; each edge drawn twice (soft wide glow and thin bright core) with lighter compositing so overlaps bloom
- Vertices drawn as pre-rendered radial-gradient sprites in 16 colour buckets, sized by depth
- Translucent faces filled additively per polygon and cell-by-cell mode that eases each cell in and out, brightening its edges to white and dimming the rest via precomputed edge-to-cell and face-to-cell tables
- Hover picking of the nearest projected vertex with a ringed marker, a leader-line label of its 4D coordinates and a live readout in the Shadow inset, which projects the unrotated polytope so the cube-within-cube stays legible
- Hairline SVG monoline title lettering (nine glyph paths built from lines and Bezier curves), custom styled range inputs with gradient hairline tracks and glowing dot thumbs
- Static background canvas with concentric hairline rings, degree ticks, crosshair and a seeded star field; DPR-capped, ResizeObserver-driven layout with a bottom-sheet variant for phones

==================== INTERACTION ====================
Choose a polytope from the list (or keys 1 to 5). The six sliders set the angular speed of each plane in radians per second (defaults leave XY, XZ and YZ still and turn XW, YW and ZW slowly); Reset restores the defaults and Freeze stops everything. In the default Auto mode, dragging the canvas orbits the 3D view with inertia and the view leans gently toward the pointer; with Manual switched on (key M) the automatic rotation stops and a horizontal drag rotates the XW and ZW planes while a vertical drag rotates YW, releasing with inertia. Faces (key F) adds translucent additive faces, Cell by cell (key C) highlights each cell in turn. Hover (or tap, on touch screens) near a vertex to ring it, see its original 4D coordinates on a label and in the Shadow inset. On phones the controls move to a bottom sheet with a collapsible Rotation planes section. With prefers-reduced-motion the default rotation speeds and cell cycling are slowed.
