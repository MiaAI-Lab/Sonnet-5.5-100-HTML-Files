==================== TITLE ====================
Wanderlight — Journeys Beyond

==================== DESCRIPTION ====================
A dark, cinematic landing page for a fictional space-tourism line. The hero is a floating field of five worlds painted entirely with CSS gradients: a cratered, rayed Moon; a rust Mars with polar caps, albedo seas and a canyon scar; a cracked-ice Europa with an orbiting moonlet; an amber, haze-wrapped Titan; and a banded Saturn whose ring system is built from conic and radial gradients tilted with rotateX and split into back and front halves so the globe sits inside its rings. Each world has its own slow bob and parallax depth, floating over a nebula and three depths of twinkling box-shadow stars with occasional shooting stars. The headline "Where will you wake up?" is set in a large geometric sans with pink-to-gold gradient text. A five-way planet dock swings the chosen world to the centre and enlarges it while the others recede, blur and dim; a frosted-glass card updates with count-up travel time, gravity and price, the view from the window, and a mini SVG flight arc where a tiny ship flies from Earth to the chosen world. Below the fold: three glass feature cards with animated SVG illustrations, a stats strip, a two-tone boarding pass (navy body, cream stub, dotted perforation, notched edges, hand-authored CSS barcode, holographic seal) that rewrites itself for the selected destination and can be torn, a marquee of traveller testimonials, and a horizon-arc call to action with glowing buttons and a live T-minus countdown.

==================== TECHNIQUES ====================
- Planets drawn only with CSS gradients: radial-gradient spheres, multi-layer crater/albedo/lineae textures, repeating-conic-gradient crater rays, linear-gradient gas-giant bands, elliptical arcs from radial-gradients for Europa's cracks
- Shared lighting model per sphere: a highlight radial-gradient, a terminator falloff gradient and stacked inset box-shadows (dark limb, cool cyan rim light, thin lit edge), plus a halo ring gradient for atmosphere
- Saturn's rings from a conic-gradient (lit / shadowed sides), a repeating-radial-gradient of ringlets and a gap-carving radial mask, tilted with perspective() + rotateX(73deg) and clipped into back and front halves for correct occlusion
- Nested transform layers per planet (slot travel, JS parallax, bob keyframes, scale/blur/opacity depth) so transitions are compositor-friendly; will-change only where no scaling occurs
- Three box-shadow star layers built at runtime from a seeded PRNG (density scales with viewport), each split into two twinkle groups; nebula gradients drifting slowly; CSS shooting stars
- Parallax driven by JS-set CSS variables (--mx / --my) from pointer, device orientation, or a gentle idle sway; lerped in a delta-time requestAnimationFrame loop that pauses when the hero is off-screen or the tab is hidden
- Glass UI: backdrop-filter blur, gradient hairline borders, inner highlights; animated buttons with sheen sweep and blurred glow
- SVG flight arc computed per destination (arc height and length scale with distance), draw-in dash, ship positioned with getPointAtLength and rotated to the tangent
- Count-up number tweens with cancel tokens; @property-registered colour for the smooth aura crossfade
- Boarding pass with radial-gradient mask notches, dotted perforation, hand-written repeating-linear-gradient barcode, holographic conic seal, pointer-tilt sheen and a tear-off stub
- Seamless clone-based marquee, IntersectionObserver reveals, live countdown, roving-tabindex radiogroup with arrow-key support

==================== INTERACTION ====================
Pick a world with the planet dock, the prev/next arrows, the keys 1-5, the arrow keys (roving focus inside the dock), or by clicking any receded planet: the chosen sphere flies to the centre and grows, the others shrink, blur and dim, a pulse ring radiates, and the glass card, flight arc and boarding pass all update (the pass has its own destination chips). Moving the mouse (or tilting a phone) shifts every planet and star layer by a different parallax amount; with no input the scene sways gently on its own. Hovering a receded planet un-blurs it and shows its label. The logo sends a comet across the sky. The boarding pass tilts toward the pointer with a moving sheen, and "Tear along the dotted line" rips the stub away (toggle to reattach). The testimonial strip drifts on its own and pauses on hover or focus. Scrolling reveals sections with staggered fades and the nav turns to frosted glass. With prefers-reduced-motion the bobbing, twinkle, marquee, orbiting moons, shooting stars and parallax are switched off, transitions are shortened, and the page still reads as complete.
