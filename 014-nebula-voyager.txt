==================== TITLE ====================
Nebula Voyager

==================== DESCRIPTION ====================
A full-screen WebGL flight through a volumetric emission nebula. A fragment shader raymarches warped, ridged fbm noise (26 to 36 adaptive steps per ray, jittered per pixel) into a low-resolution target; a second pass upscales it with a smoothing tap pattern and blue-noise-style dithering, composites it over deep indigo #0a0620, and adds soft blooms around three star clusters. Clouds shade from cyan wisps through magenta bodies to amber cores, lit by the nearest embedded clusters. Over it, thousands of far and near stars are drawn as additive point sprites with parallax, twinkle, radial streaking at speed and four-point diffraction spikes on the brightest, and each cluster carries a tight knot of blue-white stars. The interface is minimal: a wide light-tracked title card that dissolves into a small corner title, a thin-line reticle, fictional right ascension and declination that follow the view, a distance-travelled counter in light-years, a nearest-cluster range, a velocity readout, an autopilot indicator, and a pill selector for the Emission, Reflection and Dark Lane palettes plus a Thrust button. Film grain and a soft vignette finish the frame. Without WebGL a 2D canvas starfield drifts over CSS radial gradients tinted by the chosen palette.

==================== TECHNIQUES ====================
- Raymarched volume in GLSL: three-octave domain warp feeding four-octave fbm plus a ridged term for filaments, a large-scale envelope for voids, emission-absorption integration with growing step size and per-pixel jitter, early exit when transmittance is low, and a colour ramp keyed to density and a warp-derived hue.
- Two-pass rendering: the nebula renders into a framebuffer at 0.2 to 0.8 of CSS pixel size; the blit pass smooths it with four half-texel taps, adds an interleaved-gradient dither to hide banding, and paints cluster blooms; adaptive resolution and step count ramp up quickly on capable GPUs and back off on slow ones.
- Additive point-sprite starfield: 4,200 far stars on a sphere, 2,600 near stars in a wrapping 64-unit box for true parallax, and clustered stars with gaussian offsets; the vertex shader projects and sizes them, the fragment shader draws gaussian core, halo, axis-aligned diffraction spikes and radial streaks, and dims stars behind dense cloud using the nebula alpha.
- Periodic star-cluster lattice: cluster centres repeat every 64 units so the nearest image is always found by rounding, giving continuous embedded lights that fade to zero before they can pop.
- Camera and flight model: inertial yaw and pitch from drag velocity, banking roll, gentle hover steering, boost from wheel, pinch, Space or the Thrust button decaying back to cruise, autopilot blend after a few idle seconds.
- Palette presets that cross-fade uniforms (colour ramps, density, absorption, emission, light gain, background glow, exposure) so Emission, Reflection and Dark Lane feel like different objects.
- HUD in CSS and SVG: wide light sans title with 0.5em tracking, monospace captions, backdrop-blurred pill controls, animated reticle that spins with roll and swells with speed, fictional RA and Dec derived from the heading.
- Film grain from a canvas-generated tile animated in steps under an overlay blend, plus a radial vignette.
- Graceful degradation: shader compile or context failure logs a warning and switches to the 2D canvas starfield over CSS gradients; reduced motion slows drift and skips the intro card animation.

==================== INTERACTION ====================
Drag (mouse or touch) to steer: the sky follows your hand and keeps turning with inertia when you let go; moving the mouse without pressing nudges the view slightly. Scroll the wheel, pinch with two fingers, hold Space or ArrowUp/W, or hold the Thrust button to fly faster through the cloud; the speed eases back to cruise and the distance counter in light-years keeps rising. Arrow keys or A/D steer. Left alone for a few seconds the camera switches to autopilot and drifts on its own; any input returns to manual control. Choose Emission, Reflection or Dark Lane with the pill buttons (arrow keys move between them) or the 1, 2 and 3 keys; the colours and density cross-fade. H hides the HUD. The hint line fades a few seconds after your first flight input. With prefers-reduced-motion the drift is much slower and the animated title card is skipped.
