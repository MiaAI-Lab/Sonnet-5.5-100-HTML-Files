==================== TITLE ====================
Metro Lumen Transit Map

==================== DESCRIPTION ====================
A 1970s-modernist transit diagram on off-white #f7f5f0: five coloured lines (Red, Blue, Green, Amber, Violet) built only from horizontal, vertical and 45-degree segments with rounded corners, plus a thin grey Ring line, 27 stations in total. Ordinary stations are white circles with a coloured outline, fifteen interchanges are larger white circles with black outlines and heavier labels, names are set in a Helvetica-style grotesque, horizontally or at 45 degrees so they clear the lines. Context shapes are a light-blue river band that winds under the lines, two pale-green parks, and three tinted zone bands. The header has a bold tracked "METRO LUMEN" wordmark with an SVG roundel (red ring, blue bar, white LUMEN). A legend lists the lines; hovering or focusing an entry isolates that line by fading everything else. Hovering or focusing a station opens a white card with the station name, line chips and the next two arrivals in each direction. Clicking two stations plans a journey: a Dijkstra search over station-line states with a 4-minute interchange penalty draws the route as a thick white-cased coloured glowing path with A and B pins, and the side panel shows total minutes, changes and a numbered step list. Small white train dots run along every line, easing in and out of each station with a short dwell; the Rush hour toggle doubles the trains, shortens headways, raises the interchange penalty and pulses the interchange rings.

==================== TECHNIQUES ====================
- Hand-laid geometry: every line is a list of grid points; a roundPath() helper turns them into quadratic-corner paths (closed for the Ring); stations sit exactly on segments; labels placed by a compass code (e, w, n, s, and 45-degree ne/nw/se/sw rotations) with a paper-coloured halo stroke.
- SVG map built by JS from one data table (lines, station coordinates, label placement), with static context shapes (zones, river with textPath label, parks) written in the markup.
- Station arc positions found once by sampling getPointAtLength; trains move along each line's path with getPointAtLength, using a cosine ease per segment plus dwell, bouncing at terminals and circulating both ways on the Ring.
- Dijkstra over (station, line) nodes with travel times from segment length and a transfer penalty; legs merged into steps; route drawn by cloning the line path with stroke-dasharray and dashoffset so only the travelled stretch shows (with wrap handling for the loop), plus a drop-shadow glow.
- Legend isolation via data-l attributes and a dim class; arrivals derived from wall-clock time and per-line headways.
- Keyboard access: an HTML button overlaid at each station (percentage positioned) with descriptive aria-labels, visible focus ring, Enter selects, Escape clears.
- Responsive: two-column layout with a sticky side panel; on narrow screens the panel stacks and the map becomes an intentional horizontal scroller with larger tap targets.
- Motion: cards and dimming fade, interchange pulse rings under Rush hour, reduced motion slows trains and removes pulses; loop pauses when the tab is hidden.

==================== INTERACTION ====================
Hover (or Tab to) a line in the legend to isolate it. Hover or focus any station for its card with next arrivals. Click (or press Enter on) a first station to set A, then a second to set B; the route, minutes and steps appear, a third click starts a new journey, clicking A again removes it. Clear route (button, link or Escape) resets. The Rush hour toggle adds trains and pulses and re-plans with the higher interchange penalty. On touch, tap stations and scroll the map sideways.
