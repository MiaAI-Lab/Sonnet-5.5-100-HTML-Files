==================== TITLE ====================
Maison Valcourt, Calibre One

==================== DESCRIPTION ====================
A silent, expensive, patient landing page for the fictional watchmaker Maison Valcourt, set on near-black with champagne, ivory and smoked-bronze accents. The hero is a centre-stage inline-SVG watch: a champagne-gold case with lugs and a leather strap that fades into the dark, a satin sunburst chapter ring, two-tone applied indexes with soft cast shadows, a three-band rose-engine guilloché of wavy antiphase rings, blued dauphine hands, a fine sweeping seconds hand that shows the visitor's real local time, a power-reserve subdial that the crown can wind, and a moon-phase aperture that shows the real age of the moon. "Calibre" and an italic "One" are set in huge, light Didone-stack lettering on either side of the case and reveal letter by letter. Scrolling pins a six-layer exploded view of the movement (hands, dial, bridges, balance, going train, mainplate) that tilts, yaws and separates with the scroll, with meshing gears and a beating balance. Below come a horizontal specification list drawn with hairlines, a "Three hours of the day" dial selector that recolours the SVG between champagne, midnight and verdigris, and a "Request a private viewing" form with underline inputs, ending in a footer with an outlined VALCOURT wordmark. Everything is drawn with inline SVG, CSS and two small canvases; there are no external files.

==================== TECHNIQUES ====================
- Layered inline SVG watch (strap, lugs, case, dial base, details, hands) stacked as separate compositor layers inside one tilting wrapper; a second dial instance in the dial section re-uses the same artwork through <use> so both recolour and keep time together.
- Rose-engine guilloché: three concentric bands of ~60 antiphase sine-modulated rings (44, 72 and 108 waves) computed once into typed arrays and stroked onto small canvases as a dark pass plus an offset highlight pass; redrawn on resize with the device-pixel ratio capped at 2.
- Conic-gradient glint rings, masked to the bezel with a radial mask, rotate to follow the pointer angle and the scroll offset (with slow idle drift); a softer conic sheen and a pointer-following crystal highlight are moved with transform-only updates so nothing repaints.
- Hands driven by spring physics: an 8 Hz beat with an under-damped elastic overshoot on the seconds hand, an intro that starts the hands at the classic 10:09:36 pose and settles them on the real time with a tiny back-out overshoot, and a spring-damped power-reserve needle; the crown's knurling rolls when wound.
- Moon phase computed from the synodic month (29.53 days from a known new moon); the aperture disc slides a gold moon across a starry sky and a live caption names the phase and day.
- Exploded movement: a 340vh sticky track whose scroll progress drives explode distance, oblique squash (0.94 to 0.46) and yaw of six SVG layers; wheels are generated from tooth counts on a shared module with correct tooth-phase meshing so they visibly interlock; balance, hairspring breathing, pallet fork and a stepping escape wheel animate; dashed arbor guide lines connect the layers and leader lines plus HTML labels fade in per layer; pointer x adds parallax and yaw.
- Dial colour selector: colour tokens are tweened in JavaScript and written to CSS custom properties, so the gradients, engraving lines, indexes, hands, glow and even the ambient page light change smoothly together; controls are ARIA radio groups with arrow-key support and stay in sync between the hero swatches, the finishes list and the form.
- Letter-by-letter headline reveal (each glyph split into a span with staggered blur, rise and rotate), hairlines that draw themselves with scaleX/scaleY transitions triggered by IntersectionObserver, ghost roman numerals with scroll parallax.
- Almost imperceptible film grain: an inline SVG feTurbulence filter applied to a fixed rect (rasterised once as its own layer), plus a soft vignette.
- Custom circle cursor that follows with easing, grows over interactive elements and shows tiny captions (Wind, Select, Drag, Send); hidden on touch devices.
- Horizontal specification scroller with scroll-snap, mouse drag-to-scroll, progress hairline and hairline dividers that grow in sequence; underline form fields with a growing focus line, floating tracked labels, inline validation and an animated seal on the thank-you panel.

==================== INTERACTION ====================
Move the pointer over the hero and the whole watch tilts a few degrees toward it, the bezel glint swings to the pointer's angle, and a soft highlight slides across the crystal; scrolling also turns the glint. The small circle cursor follows the mouse and swells over links, buttons, swatches and inputs, with tiny captions such as Wind, Select and Drag. Click (or tap, or focus and press Enter on) the crown at three o'clock to wind it: the knurling rolls, a ring pings and the power-reserve needle springs toward 88 h; pressing W does the same. Press T or double-click the hero to replay the setting of the hands from the classic 10:09 pose. On load the hands sweep from 12 and 10:09 to the real local time with a tiny overshoot, and the seconds hand then beats eight times a second. Choose champagne, midnight or verdigris with the hero swatches, the large finish list (arrow keys work) or the form's dial menu and everything recolours over about a second and a half. Scrolling through the pinned "The movement" section takes the movement apart layer by layer and tilts the view; moving the pointer left and right adds parallax and yaw. The specification list can be dragged with the mouse, swiped on touch, or scrolled with the keyboard once focused. Submitting the viewing form validates the name and email inline, then replaces the form with a thank-you panel and a drawing seal (nothing is sent anywhere). With prefers-reduced-motion the letter reveals, tilt, glint drift, spring smoothing, gear motion and colour tween are switched off or made instant, the seconds hand ticks once a second, and the page still shows every element fully drawn.
