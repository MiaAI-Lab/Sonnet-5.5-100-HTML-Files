==================== TITLE ====================
Coral Lab Reef Simulator

==================== DESCRIPTION ====================
A full-bleed, living Gray–Scott reaction–diffusion reef that the visitor can paint into. A 320x200 grid (re-proportioned to the viewport's aspect at about 64,000 cells, so portrait phones get a tall grid) of two chemicals, A and B, is integrated in Float32Array typed arrays with the classic Laplacian (centre -1, edges 0.2, corners 0.05), Da 1.0 and Db 0.5, ten steps per frame, on a wrapping torus. The B field and its gradient are rendered through the coral-reef ramp (deep teal #06303a, turquoise #17a2a2, peach #ffb590, coral #ff6f59, cream #fff1de) with pseudo-3D emboss lighting: a WebGL2 fragment shader samples the field with cubic B-spline interpolation so the low-resolution simulation is scaled up smoothly into glossy, tubular coral with a specular light that follows the pointer, cast shadows on the deep-teal sea floor, animated caustics and fine grain. A floating cream panel (#fff8ef) holds the title "Coral Lab" set in a bold, soft, rounded-looking serif (heavy Bookman/Cooper-style stack thickened with a text stroke), the field notes "Turing patterns · Gray–Scott", five icon preset chips, feed and kill sliders, a small parameter map (x = k, y = f) with coloured behaviour zones drawn from the saddle-node curve, Seed and Erase tools, brush size and tempo sliders, Pause, Reset and an Atlas mode. The reef opens as a golden-angle seeded time-lapse that is already mature at first paint and keeps evolving. The presets were verified in a numerical model and on screen; Mitosis, Worms, Mazes and Coral use the briefed values, while Spots was moved from f 0.035 / k 0.065 (which merely duplicated Mitosis) to f 0.030 / k 0.062, which gives a distinct lattice of larger, stable dots.

==================== TECHNIQUES ====================
- Gray–Scott kernel in typed arrays: padded Float32Array ping-pong buffers with a wrapped ghost ring (no modulo in the inner loop), clamped to 0–1, about 0.8 ms per step at 320x200; steps per frame are budgeted by measured cost (Tempo 1–12, default 10) with a fast time-lapse at start and after each preset.
- Per-cell feed and kill arrays: a smooth, tileable low-frequency noise (about +/-0.0009 f and +/-0.00028 k) gives the reef organic variety; Atlas mode replaces it with a gradient (f rises up the page, k grows to the right) so every behaviour appears at once, with a pointer probe reading f and k.
- WebGL2 renderer: RGBA16F texture (B, dB/dx, dB/dy, A) re-uploaded each frame, REPEAT wrapping for the torus, a one-triangle draw, cubic B-spline sampling through four linear taps, Lambert plus tight specular from a point light that follows the pointer (or drifts on its own), cast shadow taps, joltz0r-style caustics on the floor, hash grain; adaptive render scale if frames are slow.
- Fallback 2D-canvas renderer (same palette LUT and emboss maths on the CPU, smoothed upscale) for browsers without WebGL2, with context-loss handling and no console noise.
- Palette ramp built from the five brief colours with chained smoothstep mixes, mapped from B (0 to about 0.4), plus CSS vignette overlay.
- Presets reseed the water and regrow in time-lapse (patterns are multistable, so this is what makes each one characteristic): Coral 60 seeds, Mitosis 45, Worms 150, Mazes 70, Spots 70; f and k glide to the target with exponential easing and the map marker and sliders follow.
- Parameter map: pre-rendered canvas coloured by the distance from the saddle-node curve k = sqrt(f)/2 - f (solid, labyrinth and worm band, spot band, still water), numbered preset dots, live crosshair, drag or arrow-key travel, atlas window outline.
- Brush system: soft-edged wrapped discs, interpolated along the stroke, seed or erase (Shift, right button or Erase tool), rising bubbles when painting and ambient bubbles from living coral, a DOM ring cursor sized to the brush, big serif on-canvas caption when a preset is chosen.
- Rounded serif title: heavy Bookman-style stack with -webkit-text-stroke and paint-order so serifs soften into a chunky, friendly wordmark.
- Responsive panel: floating card on desktop, bottom sheet with a horizontally scrolling chip row on phones, foldable with a chevron (H key); prefers-reduced-motion pre-rolls a mature reef, slows the tempo, freezes the light and disables caption and bubbles.

==================== INTERACTION ====================
Drag anywhere on the water (mouse or touch) to seed new coral; hold Shift, right-click, or choose Erase to wipe it away. Chips 1 to 5 (or keys 1 to 5) switch to Coral, Mitosis, Worms, Mazes or Spots, regrowing the reef in time-lapse with a large caption. The feed and kill sliders and the parameter map (click, drag, or focus it and use the arrow keys, Shift for bigger steps) change the chemistry live so the existing pattern morphs across behaviour boundaries. Seed and Erase pick the tool, Size sets the brush radius ([ and ] keys), Tempo sets the steps per frame. Pause (Space or P), Reset (R), Atlas (A) and hide or show the panel (H or the chevron) complete the controls; E toggles the erase tool. With the canvas focused, arrow keys move a virtual brush, Enter seeds and Backspace erases. The specular light follows the pointer and slowly orbits when it rests. When the tab is hidden the loop stops. Reduced motion gives a mature, slower, calmer reef with no bubbles or captions.
