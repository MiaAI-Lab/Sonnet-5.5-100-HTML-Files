Lung Fish

Loud, damp & (slightly) improving

an interview by Dee, conducted in a car park behind The Sump at 1 a.m., over chips.

Q. How did Lung Fish start?

MARGO: We met at a bus stop. A burnt-out one. Nobody hurt, the bus was late anyway. Dev had a bass with two strings and a lot of opinions.

DEV: Three strings. I’ve been busy.

Q. How do you write a song?

MARGO: Rev shouts a word, Kit hits something, I find the note that hurts most. That’s a song. Usually ninety seconds. We’re not monsters.

Q. Why a zine, in this economy?

DEV: Nobody can algorithm a staple.

Q. Best gig ever?

KIT: The laundrette. Tuesday, nine people, one asleep. The dryer kept perfect time. We asked it to join the band.

Q. Worst?

MARGO: A wedding. Not ours.

Q. Advice for new bands?

MARGO: Play the wrong notes with total confidence.

REV: And learn where the fuse box is.

“Nobody can algorithm a staple.”

— Dev, bass (three strings)

“Play the wrong notes with total confidence.”

— Margo, guitar, voice

how to start a band (or a zine, or a mess)

Eight rules for idiots

  1. Learn three chords. That’s the curriculum.
  2. No amp? Buy a cheap one. Blame the room.
  3. Book your own gigs: church halls, laundrettes, garages, roofs. Ask nicely. Then ask anyway.
  4. Split the door money equally. Write it down. Pin it up.
  5. The photocopier is an instrument. Learn its moods.
  6. Staple, don’t glue. Glue has opinions.
  7. If it sounds bad, do it louder. If it still sounds bad, call it a genre.
  8. Leave the venue cleaner than you found it, so they let you back.

This is a chord. This is another. This is a third. Now form a band.
