Courtyard

The sundial that runs six minutes late

Every guest asks. Our gardener has a theory involving the church, the tram and the ancient art of not caring.

The dial in our courtyard was cut in 1926 for a slightly different latitude, which is why it has been politely wrong ever since. We stopped correcting it years ago. At what the stone calls noon, the kitchen goes quiet for exactly one minute, and someone always says the same thing: it is late somewhere.

Words by Ilaria · 4 min
02

The concierge map

Seven doors worth knocking on

A short, opinionated walk from the lobby: the baker who will not sell after ten, the shop that mends umbrellas, the church you should try to enter.

Turn left at the lemon tree, then keep the sea on your right shoulder. The second door with a green shutter is Nino’s. He opens at six, sells out by nine, and considers the words “to go” a personal insult. Eat standing up, as intended, and do not ask for oat milk.

The front desk · 6 min
03

From the kitchen

Long tables, slow bread

Our sourdough is fed twice a day and named after a cousin. This is how a jam jar outlived three head chefs.

The starter arrived in a jam jar in 2011 and has never left the building. It has survived a flood, two renovations and a chef who tried to make it more efficient. Feed it at dawn, feed it at dusk, and it repays you with a crust that sings when you open the oven door.
