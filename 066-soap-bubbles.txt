==================== TITLE ====================
Thin Film

==================== DESCRIPTION ====================
A full-screen, quietly cinematic study of nine large soap bubbles drifting through a softly blurred, sunlit room. A single WebGL fragment shader paints the whole scene: a warm cream wall (#efe7dc fading to #d9cbb8) with a slanted patch of sunlight crossed by window-frame shadows, drifting bokeh discs and an out-of-focus potted plant silhouette, then shades every bubble as an analytic sphere. Each film is physically inspired: thickness is a base value (set by the Film thickness slider) minus a gravity term that makes the top thinner and the bottom thicker, plus swirling domain-warped noise, and the reflected colour is sin-squared interference evaluated per red, green and blue wavelength on the optical path 2·n·t·cos(refracted angle). Fresnel weighting keeps the centre nearly clear and the rims bright; the front face mirrors a mullioned window soft-box and the back face mirrors an inverted twin, with tiny specular glints. Bubbles wobble into ellipses, bump into each other, and slip away from the pointer. A tiny THIN FILM caption in wide-tracked caps carries an italic physics note (Interference of light on a 500 nm film, updating with the slider) and a two-line explanation. A frosted dock at the bottom holds a Studio button (switches to a dark studio where soft-boxes and vivid irises pop) and the spectrum-coloured Film thickness slider. Without WebGL the page falls back to pastel CSS bubbles with hue-rotate animation that still pop on click.

==================== TECHNIQUES ====================
- Single full-screen-triangle WebGL fragment shader with uniform arrays (position/radius/seed, wobble axes and angle, pop state) for up to 12 analytically shaded bubbles, loop-bound safe for GLSL ES 1.00, highp with mediump fallback and a uniform-count guard
- Thin-film interference: reflectance sin^2(pi * 2 n t cos(theta_t) / lambda) for lambda = 650/550/450 nm, n = 1.33, refracted angle from Snell's law, slight saturation lift, black-film clamp at the thin top
- Film thickness field: base nm from the slider, minus 215 nm times the vertical coordinate (gravity drainage), plus warped sine-swirl noise that flows over time; independent phase for the back surface
- Fresnel-weighted (Schlick-style) reflection, front-face and inverted back-face environment lookups, a gnomonic-projection soft-rectangle window with mullion bars, a three-light studio rig, tiny power-law specular glints and soft-knee highlight compression
- Procedural blurred interior: vertical wall gradient, sheared and rotated sun parallelogram with window-frame shadow, floating bokeh discs, a nine-leaf swaying plant and pot built from soft leaf profiles, vignette and per-pixel dither against banding
- CPU physics: wandering home attractors, curl-like buoyancy noise, drag, speed cap, soft wall bounces, mass-weighted circle collisions with restitution, damped elliptical wobble spring driven by impacts and pointer pushes
- Pop effect: a film hole grows from the exact click point in the shader with a bright retracting rim, plus a 2D canvas overlay with expanding double rings and gravity droplets; population refills by queued bubbles rising from below
- Press-and-hold blowing: a wand ring is drawn at the pointer while a bubble grows to a random target size, then detaches with pointer momentum; keyboard shortcuts B, P and S for blow, pop and studio
- Adaptive render scale from a frame-time monitor (capped by devicePixelRatio), visibility-aware rAF loop with clamped delta time, optional ?hq flag for fixed full resolution
- Spectrum-gradient slider track computed from the same interference formula; CSS fallback with radial and conic gradients, blur and animated hue-rotate; reduced-motion slows drift, swirl and wobble

==================== INTERACTION ====================
Move the mouse (or drag a finger) and nearby bubbles are gently pushed away, with the pointer's velocity dragging them along and shape wobble rippling through them. Click or tap a bubble to pop it: a hole opens in the film from the point you hit, a double ring bursts outward and tiny droplets fall; a new bubble rises from the bottom of the screen a couple of seconds later. Press and hold on empty space to blow: a little wand ring appears, a bubble swells at the pointer, and letting go (or reaching full size) releases it with your hand's momentum; keep holding to blow more, up to the shader limit (the oldest pops if the room is full). The Film thickness slider (200 to 900 nm, keyboard and touch friendly) eases the colour bands from silvery gold through magenta and green to pastel pinks while the caption number follows it. The Studio button (or the S key) cross-fades the room into a dark studio with soft-box reflections and saturated irises, and back to Daylight. B blows a bubble and P pops one from the keyboard. On touch devices a tap pops and a hold blows; the hint text adapts. With prefers-reduced-motion the drift, swirl, wobble and plant sway run at a fraction of their speed and droplet counts are halved. If WebGL is unavailable or the context is lost, pastel CSS bubbles float and hue-cycle; clicking one pops it and another appears later, and the sliders and Studio button still work.
